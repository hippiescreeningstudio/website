This August, we return to Neues Rottmann with Ang Lee's _Lust, Caution_, adapted from Eileen Chang's razor-thin novella of espionage and desire.

**Synopsis**

Hong Kong, 1938. Wong Chia Chi, a shy freshman displaced by the war, joins a student drama troupe whose patriotic plays soon spill off the stage. The group resolves to assassinate Mr. Yee, a senior recruiter for the secret police of the Japanese-backed puppet government, and Chia Chi is handed the role of her life: "Mrs. Mak", the poised, idle wife of a trader, sent to move close to him.

The amateur plot collapses. Four years later, in occupied Shanghai, the mission is revived under professional handlers, and she has to put the costume back on. What follows is a long, patient game played across mah-jong tables, tailors' fittings, cafés and hotel rooms, in which Yee's paranoid cruelty and Chia Chi's borrowed intimacy grow harder and harder to tell apart from something neither of them can afford to name.

Shot by Rodrigo Prieto in amber and shadow, scored by Alexandre Desplat, Ang Lee's most physically explicit film is also his most tightly controlled: a study of collaboration in every sense of the word, and of a woman who plays a part until there is nothing left underneath it.

**About the Director**

**Ang Lee 李安** (b. 1954, Pingtung, Taiwan) studied theatre in Taiwan and film at NYU before making his first features in the early 1990s. His work moves between languages and genres with unusual ease — _Eat Drink Man Woman_, _Sense and Sensibility_, _Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon_, _Brokeback Mountain_, _Life of Pi_ — and has twice won him the Academy Award for Best Director. _Lust, Caution_ brought him his second Golden Lion at Venice, two years after _Brokeback Mountain_.

**Awards**

Golden Lion, Venice Film Festival 2007. At the 44th Golden Horse Awards the film took seven prizes, including Best Feature Film, Best Director, Best Leading Actor for Tony Leung Chiu-wai, and Best New Performer for Tang Wei, in her first leading role.

**A note on the source**

Eileen Chang 张爱玲 worked on the novella for more than twenty years before publishing it in 1979, and it runs to barely thirty pages. Ang Lee's adaptation fills in everything Chang leaves out — the rehearsals, the waiting, the bodies — while keeping her cold final turn intact.

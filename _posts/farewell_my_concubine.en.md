This November, we screen _Farewell My Concubine_ (霸王别姬), Chen Kaige's 1993 epic, as the 50th screening of Hippie Screening Studio. It is one of the most important classics of Chinese cinema, and the first Chinese-language film to win the Palme d'Or at Cannes.

**Synopsis**

In the 1920s, an abandoned boy named Douzi is raised in a Peking opera troupe, where he is trained to play female roles. He forms a close bond with fellow apprentice Shitou, who plays the male roles. Years later, as Cheng Dieyi and Duan Xiaolou, they are celebrated stars, famous for their performance in the tragic opera _Farewell My Concubine_: Dieyi as the loyal Consort Yu, Xiaolou as the doomed hero Xiang Yu.

Dieyi's devotion to Xiaolou runs deeper than the stage, and it is strained when Xiaolou marries Juxian, a courtesan. As Japanese occupation, civil war and political upheaval reshape China across five decades, the three are pressed again and again. Their loyalties, their art and their sense of self are tested by the times they live through.

**Awards**

Shared the Palme d'Or at Cannes in 1993 with _The Piano_. It also won the Golden Globe and the BAFTA for Best Foreign Language Film, and was nominated for two Academy Awards, Best Foreign Language Film and Best Cinematography.
